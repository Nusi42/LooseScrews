# Visual quality plan: first impression

Status: in progress (package status in §3 "Package status"). Trigger: review findings after M2: the FOV feels wrong; openings look passable up close, but the ship does not fit through; the ships look too basic; the effects look dated and sparse; the textures are too simple and switch hard between polygons. Requirement: the first impression must be strong, because players rarely give a game a second chance.

This plan treats visual quality as a first-class track that runs alongside the M3 waves (`docs/milestone-3-plan.md`). It has three parts: findings with evidence (§1), the immediate fix for openings that look passable but block the ship (§2), and a prioritized overhaul as work packages (§3–§5).

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Openings that look passable but block the ship: the cause is the collision distance, not the FOV

The sim's ship collision (`crates/ls-sim/src/ship.rs`, `move_sphere_with`) treats `world_sdf(p)` as the true distance to the rock: a ship touches when `sdf < radius` and is pushed out until `sdf = radius`. The generator only promises a **lower bound** on the distance (`contracts.md`: "1-Lipschitz (a true distance or a lower bound on it)"). The cavern field is gradient noise scaled by its worst-case slope (`CHAMBER_SCALE = 28 / NOISE_LIPSCHITZ`, `TUNNEL_SCALE = 24 / NOISE_LIPSCHITZ`, `NOISE_LIPSCHITZ = 3.6`, `crates/ls-worldgen/src/generator.rs`). The typical slope is about 1, so the field reports about 0.3 of the real distance. The ship stops against an invisible shell that is 2–3× its radius away from the rock you see.

Measured with a scratch probe (outside the repo) that compares `ChunkStore::eval` (what the sim uses) with the distance to the surface-nets mesh the client draws. It sampled 20 000 points per world in the 3×3×3 chunks around the room centre:

| World (seed, enclosure, density) | median \|∇sdf\| near rock | Visible gap to the wall when a Tinker (r = 0.5) touches: p10 / p50 / p90 | Points where the mesh says "fits" (≥ 0.5 u clear) but the sim blocks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7, 50, 50 (caverns) | 0.28 | 1.09 / **1.66** / 2.92 u | 81 % |
| 42, 50, 50 (caverns) | 0.35 | 0.85 / **1.35** / 1.90 u | 35 % |
| 99, 70, 60 (caverns/mines blend) | 0.47 | 0.79 / **0.96** / 1.29 u | 55 % |
| 7, 20, 70 (sky + rocks) | 1.00 | 0.50 / 0.65 / 0.85 u | 8 % |
| 7, 100, 30 (mine shafts, exact boxes) | 1.00 | 0.40 / 0.50 / 0.64 u | 3 % |

Read the table like this: in a typical cave world a Tinker, whose ship is 1 u long, needs a hole about **3.3 u across** (2 × 1.66), not 1 u. A Hauler (r = 0.65) needs about 4.8 u. All four hulls are affected (Skitter 0.4 → median visible gap 1.36 u, Tug 0.55 → 1.92 u). Mine-shaft worlds are fine because their box SDFs are exact. That matches the review finding: openings look passable from up close and are not.

The same bound distance is used everywhere a sphere meets terrain: scrape, paint touching (`paint_touching`), projectiles with a radius (rockets hit an invisible shell early), debris and decoy spheres. Sphere-traced raycasts stay correct, but they take about 3.6× more steps than they would on a true distance.

**The fix works.** Replacing `d` with the first-order distance estimate `d / |∇d|` (only when `d < radius`, so open flight costs nothing extra) brings the visible gap at contact to the hull radius: median 0.49 u for r = 0.5 (p10 0.40, p90 0.54) in seed 7 caverns, 0.50 u in seed 42, 0.45 u in seed 99. Exact-SDF worlds are unchanged.

Second-order contributors:
- **Surface nets on a 1 u grid** draw the rock 0.15–0.4 u from the true surface (world 7/20/70: +0.16 u median, +0.39 u p95). Surface nets also round off convex bumps and box edges (mine shafts: −0.1 to +0.36 u), so bumps that stick into a passage look smaller than they are. §3 VQ3a reduces this.
- **FOV and lack of a size reference.** `web/src/game/camera.ts`: vertical FOV 75°, which is 107° horizontal at 16:9 and 122° at 21:9 (fixed vertical, so ultrawide gets wider). The eye sits at the centre of the collision sphere and nothing on screen shows how big the ship is. A Descent-like game needs a size cue: a hull or nose frame, or a reticle that shows the ship's cross-section at the distance of the surface ahead. 107° is at the wide end and stretches the periphery, which makes nearby openings read larger at the screen edges. The near plane (0.05 u) and far plane (600 u) are fine: the eye never gets closer than 0.4 u to rock.

### 1.2 Ships and props: too basic

- `web/src/renderer/primitives.ts` `shipMesh()`: **8 flat triangles**, a dart. All four ship types (Skitter, Tinker, Hauler, Tug) use this same mesh, scaled by hitbox radius (`scene.ts addShips`). `ShipState.shipType` is available but not used for the look. There is no engine glow, cockpit, lights, panel detail or team livery apart from a flat tint. See `target/browser-smoke/mech-decoy.png`: the decoy reads as a tiny orange arrowhead.
- Projectiles, pickups and wrecks are 8–60-triangle primitives (octahedron bolt, box crate, icosphere orbs) with the same Lambert lighting.

### 1.3 Terrain: simple textures, hard switches between polygons

From `web/src/renderer/shaders.ts` (`TERRAIN_WGSL`) and the screenshots (`practice-scene.png`, `online-rocket.png`, `online-shield.png`, `mech-charge.png`, `mech-decoy.png`):
- **Blocky "texture"**: `grain(p) = hash3(floor(p * 0.5))`, a constant random shade per 2 u cube. That is the Minecraft-like checkerboard on every wall. Hard rock glints use `floor(p * 4)` cubes. `hash3` is `fract(sin(dot(p, k)) * 43758)`, and on GPUs `sin` loses precision at level-scale arguments (up to about 16 000). That causes the diagonal streak and moiré patterns on the big walls in `online-rocket.png`.
- **Hard triangle-shaped colour patches**: the vertex attribute that carries material, paint and surface state is `@interpolate(flat)`, so one vertex decides the material of the whole triangle. Material borders (weak, rock, hard), paint edges and hot or charged patches show up as sawtooth triangles (the light/grey patches in `practice-scene.png`). This is the main "hard switch between polygons".
- **Faceted lighting**: surface-nets vertex normals are the gradient of the trilinear interpolant inside one cell (`mesh.rs`, `trilinear_gradient(&c, l)`). That gradient is not continuous across cells, so neighbouring triangles get visibly different shades on smooth rock.
- **Lighting**: Lambert sun, hemisphere ambient and a camera headlight. No specular, no ambient occlusion, no shadows, no dynamic lights from shots or explosions. Caves are lit like a flat-shaded 90s engine.
- **No LOD popping in practice**: the stream radius is 224 u and exp2 fog (density 0.012) is 95 % opaque at about 173 u, so new chunks appear inside fog. Remeshed chunks (craters) swap in a single frame, which is acceptable once explosions cover them (VQ5).

### 1.4 Output pipeline and effects: dated look, few visual effects

- **No HDR, no tonemapping, no gamma**: the renderer draws straight into the swapchain (`getPreferredCanvasFormat()` is `bgra8unorm`, which is not sRGB) and writes linear lighting values unconverted. Mid-tones come out too dark and muddy, and anything brighter than 1 clips to flat white (emissive is `color * 1.5`).
- **No anti-aliasing** (sample count 1), no bloom, no AO, no colour grading, no motion feedback.
- **"Effects" are scaled meshes**: an explosion is a translucent icosphere (`addEffects`: `sphere` with `opacity 0.6 * strength`), a trail is the bolt mesh stretched along Z, the Leech Arc is two stretched bolts, and the shield is a translucent sphere. There is **no particle system**: no sparks, smoke, fire, debris bursts, muzzle flashes, engine exhaust, impact flashes, dust motes or speed streaks. The screen-space layer (`ui/effects.ts`) does a whiteout, banners and a cloak edge in DOM/CSS.
- **Backdrop**: open space is a flat clear colour (`fogColor` 0.05/0.06/0.09). Open-sky worlds have no stars, nebula or sky gradient.
- **Performance headroom**: the smoke screenshots show 60 fps (vsync-capped) with 150–260 k triangles and 55–110 chunks drawn. The current frame does almost no GPU work, so the budget below is available for the overhaul.

### 1.5 What a modern WebGPU 6DOF arena shooter does (target)

Descent-likes live in dark enclosed spaces, so the look depends on **light**: coloured dynamic lights from every shot and explosion (Descent's signature), emissive materials with bloom, and AO that gives rock its form. For the rest of the bar:
- HDR rendering with filmic tonemapping (AgX or ACES) and sRGB output; MSAA 4× or TAA.
- Bloom on emissives; light colour grading per world type.
- Smooth-shaded terrain with triplanar rock materials: albedo variation and detail normals at 2–3 scales, strata, cavity/AO darkening, and specular on hard rock and metal veins. Smooth blends between materials and paint.
- GPU particles for every event: muzzle flashes, tracers with ribbon trails, impact sparks and dust, fireball and smoke explosions with shockwave and debris, engine exhaust that reacts to thrust and boost, hull-damage smoke, shield ripples and pickup sparkle. Ambient dust motes give motion parallax and a sense of speed.
- Ships of a few hundred to 2 000 triangles each, with distinct silhouettes per class, team livery, glowing engines, cockpit canopy and blinking nav lights.
- Camera feel: sensible FOV with a setting, a slight FOV kick on boost, hit shake and a damage vignette.

Everything above runs well within a 60 fps budget on mid-range GPUs in WebGPU (instanced draws, compute-driven particles, one bloom mip chain).

## 2. Immediate fix: fit and scale (do first)

### VQ0 Contact distance (sim; **not** parallel-safe, touches `crates/ls-sim`)

**Status: done (WIN7, on top of M3 A1).** `ls_sim::terrain::{surface_estimate, sphere_contact, sphere_contact_distance}` (the plan's `contact_distance`, split so `move_sphere_with` gets distance and normal from one gradient). Used by `move_sphere_with` (ships, debris, decoys), `paint_touching` and the scrape gap. Projectile terrain hits are sphere-traced rays (no terrain radius), so they needed no change. Measured with `crates/ls-game/tests/contact_fit.rs` (cavern seeds 7 and 42 at 50/50, seed 99 at 70/60, all four hulls): median gap to the true surface at contact went from 0.87–2.20 u to within 0.003 u of the hull radius (Euclidean p10 at least radius − 0.01 u). A Tinker flies through a 1.2 u tunnel and a Hauler through a 1.5 u tunnel whose walls report 0.28 of the distance. Cost: one Tinker step against a cave wall 3.9 µs (6.3 µs before, the old path evaluated `world_normal` separately and needed more push iterations), 0.6 µs in open flight; contact-heavy 8-ship cave tick p99 0.04 ms (0.05 ms before). `REPLAY_GOLDEN_HASH` is unchanged: the replay arena's field is exact (slope 1), so the estimate equals the bound bit for bit there; the new `ship::tests::bound_field_contact_golden_hash` pins the bound-field path on native and wasm32. Rides on A1's `SIM_BEHAVIOR_VERSION` 5. Contract: `docs/architecture/contracts.md` "Terrain distance". The bot nav follow-up is noted on A5 in `docs/milestone-3-plan.md`. Bots: nav sweep, room play and the arsenal match are green with 0 desyncs; the arsenal match moved to the next share-code seed (`..._0002`), because on the old one no bot happened to drop a mine after the trajectories changed (the old seed fired a single mine before; the seed after next fails the reconcile bound with and without VQ0).

- **Change**: add `contact_distance(terrain, p, d)` in `crates/ls-sim/src/terrain.rs`. It returns `d` when `d >= r_query` (the bound already proves the sphere is clear, so open flight costs nothing). Otherwise it returns `d / max(|∇d|, 0.2)`, using the central-difference gradient that `world_normal` already evaluates (fold the two calls together). Use it in `move_sphere_with` (contact test and push-out `radius - d_est`), `paint_touching`, the scrape gap, projectile-vs-terrain tests with a radius, and debris/decoy spheres. `world_sdf` stays the 1-Lipschitz bound for sphere tracing, so `contracts.md` keeps its rule; add one paragraph there: "sphere contact uses the first-order estimate `d/|∇d|`".
- **Determinism**: `det`-safe ops only (sqrt is IEEE-exact). Bump `SIM_BEHAVIOR_VERSION` and re-bless `REPLAY_GOLDEN_HASH`. **Schedule it with M3 A1**, which already bumps `SIM_BEHAVIOR_VERSION` to 5 and re-blesses, so there is one re-bless window and no extra protocol churn. Either the A1 package takes it, or the lead lands it on WIN1 immediately before A1 starts.
- **Bots**: `ls-bots` navigation and `ClearanceGrid` keep using the bound (conservative), so bots stay safe and humans gain the room. Add a follow-up note to A5 (bot nav cache) so it can switch clearance to the estimate later.
- **Acceptance**:
  - a test in `ls-sim` that builds a ChunkStore (seed 7, sliders 50/50) and probes contact points: median visible gap at contact within ±0.1 u of the hull radius for all four hulls, p10 ≥ radius − 0.15 (no visible clipping into rock);
  - a scenario test that flies a Tinker through a carved tunnel of diameter 1.2 u in a cavern world, and a Hauler through 1.5 u;
  - existing ship tests still pass; replay hash re-blessed with the reason documented;
  - `cargo test -p ls-bots --release` arsenal match unchanged (0 desyncs).
- **Cost**: at most 6 extra SDF evaluations per substep and only while touching rock. Measure with the arsenal match tick p99 (must stay < 4 ms per room).

### VQ1 Camera and scale cues (client; parallel-safe)

- **FOV setting**: switch from a fixed vertical FOV to a fixed **horizontal** FOV (Hor+ clamped so 21:9 does not exceed 115°). Default **100° horizontal** (66.7° vertical at 16:9). Add a slider (80–120°) in the start-screen settings, stored in `localStorage`. Add a boost FOV kick of +4° eased over 250 ms.
- **Size cue**: a minimal view-space **hull frame** (canopy struts and nose silhouette of the selected ship type at the screen edges, drawn after terrain with its own depth range, lit by the scene). Add a **fit reticle**: a thin ring around the crosshair whose screen radius is the ship's radius projected at the distance of the surface straight ahead (one raycast per frame). When the ring is bigger than the opening you are aiming at, you won't fit. The ring fades in only within 12 u of a surface.
- **Files**: `web/src/game/camera.ts`, new `web/src/renderer/hullFrame.ts`, `web/src/ui/hud.ts` (ring), `web/src/ui/startScreen.ts` (setting). Needs a `raycast(origin, dir, max)` export on the ls-wasm `Game`, an additive one-function change in `crates/ls-wasm/src/lib.rs` (shared file, merged after A2).
- **Acceptance**: FOV unit tests (Hor+ math at 4:3, 16:9 and 21:9, clamp); the browser smoke takes the screenshot `fit-tunnel.png` at a fixed pose in front of a 1.2 u tunnel with the ring visible; frame time +0.1 ms or less.

## 3. Visual overhaul: work packages

Rules for every package:
- Never touch `crates/ls-sim`, `crates/ls-net` or `crates/ls-server`. Visuals are client-only and never change replay hashes.
- **Frame budget**, at 1080p on the reference mid GPU (GTX 1660 / RX 6600 / Apple M1 class), measured by VQ8's GPU timestamps: whole frame ≤ **8 ms GPU** at "high"; integrated GPU (Iris Xe class) ≤ **14 ms** at "medium". Each package states its own share below and adds its quality-preset switches to the single `RenderQuality` object (low / medium / high, auto-picked from measured GPU time during the first 5 s).
- **Before/after screenshots**: every package adds its fixed-pose shots to the VQ8 gallery and attaches the before/after pairs to its merge note. The lead sends them for the phone review (see `.claude/memory/next-steps.md`).
- Tests: pure TS logic (packing, maths, effect timelines, presets) gets `node --test` unit tests. Shaders are compiled in the browser smoke run, and the smoke run fails on any WebGPU validation error.

**VQ9 as built (WIN5):** see [architecture/renderer.md](architecture/renderer.md#sun-occlusion-vq9). The package asked for cheap sun visibility, baked per vertex in the mesher or a shadow map within budget. Chosen: a **shadow map**, because a per-vertex bake cannot stay correct under edits (a crater in a ceiling opens the sun onto floors in other chunks, far beyond `DIRTY_MARGIN`; the bake would need sun rays through the whole level per vertex and cross-chunk invalidation), its 1 u resolution gives blobs instead of shaft edges, and it would cost meshing time that VQ3a already doubled. The mesher is unchanged: no golden hash, no `GENERATOR_VERSION` and no replay hash changes, remesh CPU cost unchanged.
- `web/src/renderer/sunShadow.ts` (`SunShadow`, `Renderer.sunShadow`): one orthographic `depth16unorm` 4096² map from the sun over the level (about 0.09 u per texel), back faces as casters (no acne on lit rock), region = the chunks seen so far snapped to the 32 u grid. Updates only on terrain changes: `setChunk` marks the old and new chunk extent's texel rectangles dirty (merged, at most 6); the next frame clears each rectangle by a scissored full-screen triangle and redraws the chunks overlapping it. Sealed levels (no sky, Enclosure >= 50: the level box encloses everything, also with the walls not casting, checked with a visibility debug view on caves, mines and the default world) skip the map: visibility 0, no lookups, no updates.
- Shading: `shaders/shadow.ts` `sun_visibility` (8-tap PCF of bilinear compares, normal offset, small bias), called once per fragment in `lighting.ts` `static_light` (terrain, models, translucent, hull frame); `sun_vis_last` shadows the terrain's GGX sun highlight and the models' sun highlight too (one-line edits in `terrain.ts` and `models/shading.ts`). Ships receive but do not cast.
- Volumetric shafts (`volumetric.ts`, `shaders/volumetric.ts`): half-resolution march (24 steps at "high", a third and 16 steps at "medium", off at "low") through the shadow map up to the scene depth, Henyey-Greenstein phase, added onto the resolved scene before the sky and the particles. Dust only in nearly enclosed worlds (fades in from Enclosure 37 to 50; first tuned for all worlds, which turned open space milky grey, so open and half-open worlds now keep their black space and skip the pass).
- Gallery: pose `shade` (`gallery/shadePose.ts`: a camera in rock's shadow looking at sunlit rock, scored by sunlit air along the view) and scenes `cave-sunbeam` (seed 7, Enclosure 47), `sun-shafts` (seed 11, Enclosure 45: a shaft through a rock arch into a cavern) and `dark-cave-fight` (the fight content in the shade of `cave-sunbeam`'s cavern). `--query shadows=off` renders the pre-VQ9 sun for before shots and costs; `--query shadowbench=full|chunk` times the map updates.

**VQ2 as built (WIN9):** see [architecture/renderer.md](architecture/renderer.md). Scene into `rgba16float` with MSAA 4x (resolve), bloom (6 mips at "high", 13-tap down with Karis average and a soft-knee threshold at scene radiance 1.0, tent up, added), exposure 1.4, colour grade hook (`Renderer.settings.post.grade`: ASC CDL plus saturation and a monochrome blend that can keep one hue; `GRADE_PRESETS` neutral, caves, mines, sky, lastColor), vignette 0.35, AgX with a look (power 1.3, saturation 1.25), sRGB encode in the composite shader, half-LSB dither, film grain off. Display-authored colours are linearised (frame uniforms in TS, instance colours and shader literals in WGSL); emissive instances reach 6x their colour (`emissiveGain`). `shaders.ts` is split into `shaders/frame.ts`, `lighting.ts`, `terrain.ts`, `objects.ts`, `post.ts`. `RenderQuality` in `renderer/quality.ts` (high: MSAA 4x, 6 bloom mips; medium: MSAA 4x, 4 mips; low: no MSAA, no bloom, still tonemapped), `?quality=` forces one, otherwise one automatic step down after 5 s of GPU-timed frames (median > 8 ms medium, > 14 ms low). ### VQ3a as built (WIN11)

**Status: done; the AO byte reaches the client, its shading is VQ3b's.** `ls_worldgen::surface_nets` / `surface_nets_with_sdf` (`crates/ls-worldgen/src/mesh.rs`; the game meshes with the latter through `ChunkStore::mesh_surface_nets`):
- **Snap**: one Newton step from the mass point (at most 0.5 u) along the smooth gradient. The distance comes from a tricubic (Catmull-Rom) interpolation of the grid; where it and the trilinear one differ by 0.1 u or more (`SNAP_KINK`: a crease or a feature finer than a cell, about a quarter of the vertices in caverns) the exact field `ChunkStore::eval` is read instead (sparse `RegionSampler`). Deviation from the plan's "one exact evaluation per vertex": that alone cost +1 ms per busy chunk native and doubled meshing in wasm; the hybrid keeps almost all of the accuracy.
- **Normals**: trilinear interpolation of central-difference corner gradients (continuous across cells and chunks).
- **AO**: 3 probes along the normal (0.6, 1.4, 2.5 u; classic SDF occlusion scaled to a distance by the local gradient), `Mesh::ao` one byte per vertex, `ChunkMesh.ao()` in ls-wasm, `MeshData.ao` in the web client. Not drawn yet: VQ3b multiplies the ambient term by it (a local, uncommitted shader hookup was used for the preview screenshots). Plain cave tunnels come out around 160/255, flat or convex rock 255, creases and slots darker; the shader can shape the curve.
- `SampleGrid` corner values gained a 4-corner halo (`GRID_HALO`, 41³ per chunk) read from the neighbouring chunks' stored samples; `ChunkStore::sample_grid` generates the in-level neighbours (the first mesh of a fresh store generates 27 chunks at once: on a loading screen that is one long call, ~60-70 ms native for one chunk of a lazily replayed store, was 4 ms). Corners more than one cell outside the level are the sealing walls alone (rock either way). Vertices shared by two chunks keep bit-identical positions, normals and AO (`seam_free_across_chunks`), `DIRTY_MARGIN` stays 3.5 (`craters_near_chunk_borders_keep_undirtied_neighbours`).
- Same change, to pay for it: `sample_grid` copies corner rows per chunk and walks only the stored paint and state layers; `mesh_materials` reuses a material lookup while a vertex stays within a certified radius (`MaterialRegion::at_with_radius`, same result as `at`); `web/src/game/chunks.ts` starts another chunk in a frame only if the running average chunk time still fits the budget.

- **Rock textures** (`web/src/renderer/rockTextures.ts`): at start-up a compute shader fills a 512² two-layer `rgba8unorm` texture array with tiling noise (periodic gradient noise, integer PCG hashes, no `sin`) and a second one builds the 10-level mip chain (2x2 box). Layer 0 "rock" (repeats every 9 u): height slope (rg), height (b: domain-warped fbm, ridged crests, Worley cracks on part of the cell edges), mineral vein ramp (a: warped zero lines of noise at an even width, in patches). Layer 1 "grain" (2.7 u): fine slope, grain height, speckle. Generated on the GPU in well under a frame; nothing on the CPU.
- **Terrain shader** (`shaders/terrain.ts`): triplanar (normal^4 weights; a projection nearly edge-on to the actual triangle, from `dpdx`/`dpdy`, is faded out, so the rounded normals over sharp mine-shaft creases do not smear) with the rock layer at 9 u (detail normal, cracks, cavity) and the grain layer at 2.7 u (fine normal), the rock layer's height at 31 u as the macro albedo blotch, and per-world **strata** (a 1D lookup of the rock height along a tilted axis: `TerrainParams` from `hashSeed(share code)`, tilt up to 28°, `RenderSettings.terrainSeed`). Detail normals use the surface-gradient method (no tangents). Per material: weak = sandy, crumbly, rough, stronger grain; rock = neutral with strata; hard = dark blue-grey, smoother, glossy **veins** (F0 0.55, roughness 0.18). **GGX** specular (Smith-Schlick, Schlick Fresnel) from the sun and the headlight; VQ6's dynamic lights reach the diffuse term through `light()` only. **AO**: VQ3a's byte (smoothstep 0.08-0.95, 85 %) times a cavity term from the rock height, on diffuse and specular.
- **Smooth blends** (`web/src/renderer/terrainBlend.ts`, client only): per vertex, one-hot weights (weak, hard, paint coverage, hot, charged, glow, scorch) are blurred over the mesh edges (2 passes, only within 2 edges of a triangle whose corners differ), packed with the AO into two `unorm8x4` per vertex (vertex slot 3, locations 3 and 4). The shader sharpens each weight around 0.5 with rock noise (`sharpen`), so borders are organic curves a few cm soft instead of grid staircases. Vertices within 1.5 u of a chunk face are shared with the neighbour chunk, which blurs with a different neighbourhood, so they keep their own weights (both chunks agree; the drawn weights stay continuous). Paint team and effect colour are dilated onto unpainted vertices next to paint; team and effect arrive as interpolated one-hot vectors and a power of 10 picks the dominant one, with a thin dark line where two paints meet (no mixed hue). Patterns (stripes, waves, dots) are anti-aliased and project along the smooth normal's dominant axis.
- **States**: hot = dark crust with glowing seams (cracks and an iso-line network of the relief, 4.5x orange-white in the seams), pulsing; charged = faint blue glow, small round sparks (integer hash, exact at level-scale coordinates) and flickering arcs; glow = soft green 0.9-1.4; scorch = charred and matte. All HDR (they bloom).
- **Quality**: `RenderQuality.terrainDetail` (pipeline override `DETAIL`): "low" samples only the rock layer (3 instead of 9 triplanar samples, no veins, no grain); the look is the same family, not the pre-VQ3b path (that path was the defect). Terrain pipelines are cached per MSAA count and detail; the terrain pipeline layout adds group 1 (texture array, anisotropic trilinear sampler, `TerrainParams`).
- **Gallery**: new scenes `material-border` (decor.ts `pickBorder`: the probe hit with the most materials within 2.5 u), `paint-edge` and `surface-states` (`decor.ts`: spheres of paint teams/colours or state bits on the chunk meshes where the camera looks, as the sim's edits mark vertices).
- **Measured** (gallery, RTX 5080, Chrome 154, 1920x1080, "high", terrain pass before -> after): cave close-up 0.13 -> 0.22 ms, cave tunnel 0.10 -> 0.18, open sky 0.09 -> 0.29, mine shaft 0.10 -> 0.18, material border 0.04 -> 0.09, paint edge 0.13 -> 0.24, surface states 0.17 -> 0.23; "low" 0.07-0.22. With VQ2's factor (the reference mid GPU about 10x slower in compute) that is 1.8-2.9 ms against the 2.0 ms share (2.4 with MSAA; the gallery fails above 3.0): at the share, open sky slightly above. A run on the reference class is open; if it is over, the first savings are the macro sample (3 of 9 samples) and the second GGX lobe. Blend weights on the CPU (`node`, 15 500-triangle chunk): 0.14-0.2 ms without borders, about 0.6 ms with a border across it, 2.7 ms in the pathological case of every triangle mixed (unit test bound: 64x64 grid < 8 ms); real chunks add on top of the ~2 ms wasm meshing.
- **Squeeze walls** (open item from B2 x VQ3a): not reproduced. `shell_steps_keep_normals_and_occlusion_current` (ls-game) moves a wall in 0.5 u steps on three seeds and checks after every sixth step that the dirty-chunk remeshes equal a full remesh, normals and occlusion included; it passes. The marking margins (`SHELL_MESH_BAND` 2.5, blocks expanded by 2 u, `SHELL_TERRAIN_CLAMP` 8 vs the store's clamp 6) cover the normals' reach; the farthest occlusion probe (2.5 u) could in theory see a wall up to 0.5 u beyond the band, where its contribution is about zero. No margin was widened (that would cost Squeeze remeshes). Client-side, a dirty chunk still shades its old mesh until the loader gets to it (B2 measured up to 17 frames).
- **Deviations**: the plan's "one-hot weights as a vec4 unorm8 interpolated and sharpened" became blurred weights in two `unorm8x4` (a one-cell ramp leaves grid staircases); "low" keeps a cheap variant of the new look rather than the old path; strata are an albedo tint (no ledge normals).

### VQ5 as built (WIN3)

**GPU particle system** (`web/src/renderer/particles.ts`, `shaders/particles.ts`, `spriteAtlas.ts`; details in [architecture/renderer.md](architecture/renderer.md#particles-vq5)): a storage buffer of 96-byte particles in two oldest-first rings (up to 0.7 s of life, and longer-lived smoke, dust and debris in 30 % of the slots, so a flood of sparks never evicts smoke) plus up to 4 096 immediate sprites rebuilt every frame (projectile cores, engine flames, beams, glows); 32 768 slots at "high", 16 384 at "medium", 8 192 at "low". Camera-facing billboards, velocity-stretched sprites, fixed-length ribbon sections (dimmed end-on), surface-oriented quads (shield ripples); stretched sprites turn into billboards seen along their axis. Soft particles and occlusion against the scene depth (read in the pass, no depth attachment; multisampled or not). Procedural `rg8unorm` sprite atlas baked at start-up (glow, spark, four smoke puffs, flame with a heat channel, ring, star flare, shard, streak; 128² with mips). One premultiplied blend for additive light, alpha smoke and partly opaque flames; sprites at least 32 px wide ("high"; 20 / 12 px at medium / low) render into a half-resolution target that is composited onto the scene, the rest at full resolution. Ambient dust motes: 1 500 at "high" (800 medium, none low) in a 22 u cube wrapping around the camera, streaked by its speed.

**Effect library** (`web/src/game/vfxLibrary.ts`, pure functions over a sink and an RNG) and **wiring** (`web/src/game/vfx.ts`, `Vfx.update` once per frame from `main.ts`):
- muzzle flashes per family (`energy` flare and sparks, `kinetic` flash, flame tongue and a smoke wisp, `rocket` fire burst and smoke, `lob`), for every new projectile born next to its owner (projectiles have no ids on the wire: they are matched frame to frame by owner, visual and predicted position), small and below the view for the own ship; the Railspike's muzzle-flash cue is a big flare;
- projectile cores (energy shapes: a coloured halo and a white-hot centre stretched along the flight, replacing the octahedron and orb meshes, which `scene.ts` skips with `vfx: true`), ribbon trails (overlapping streak sections with birth times along the path), rocket motors and smoke trails with flickers of fire, sparkle trails (blob, arc, paint), armed-mine blink, the Tectonic Charge's pulse;
- impacts when a tracked projectile disappears: sparks with a flare and rock dust and shards on rock, plasma bursts, paint splats, harpoon thuds, explosions for rockets, missiles, grenades and mines (radius by shape times visual scale^1.5: Bolt Rocket 1.9 u, Heavy Rocket 4.6 u); a hit next to a shielded ship ripples the bubble (a ring in its tangent plane plus sparks) instead; none inside the own ship or beyond 220 u;
- explosions: flash and star flare, a fireball of turbulent flame puffs cooling from yellow-white to deep red, smoke born inside it after the fire, sparks and embers, a shockwave ring, rock dust and shards or hull shards with burning trails; camera shake by distance and size (`Vfx.shake`, added to the cue shake in `main.ts`); shockwave cues (Tectonic Charge, Hot Reactor) explode at their size with a level-wide ring for the Tectonic;
- ships: engine flames and glow glued to every nozzle (nozzle positions found once in the VQ4 hull meshes, `engineMountsOf`: Skitter 1, Tinker 4, Hauler 4, Tug 3), an exhaust plume that grows long and blue-white at boost, none while cloaked (a faint shimmer of glints instead); a death explosion when a ship's `alive` drops (hull shards, fire, smoke), a respawn ring; damage smoke and sparks below 30 % hull, heavy smoke with fire below 15 %; fresh wrecks smoulder for 6 s;
- cue effects: blinding flash burst, swap and rewind implosions, decoy return burst, overcharge sparks, the Tectonic fuse (pulsing red glow, embers), gadget switch rings; the Leech Arc beam is a crackling two-strand electric arc with glowing ends; the timed glows of `EffectsState` are soft sprites instead of translucent spheres;
- pickups glow and sparkle and burst when taken.
- `Vfx.lights(now)` lists short lights of explosions, muzzle flashes and impacts (position, colour, reach, fading strength) for VQ6.

Data-driven: `VFX_SHAPES` maps each visual shape to its core, trail, muzzle, impact and explosion radius; the weapon's visual colour and scale tint and size it, unknown shapes look like energy bolts.

**ls-wasm** (additive): NetEvent::Hit (online) and `Event::Damaged` (practice, through `ls_game::convert::events_to_net`) become client cues of kind `CUE_HIT` = 250 (attacker, victim, impact point, damage) in `takeCues()`; tests `practice_hits_on_ships_become_hit_cues`, `online_hits_become_hit_cues`. Other ships' hull is not replicated, so the damage smoke sums the hits a ship took since it spawned against its type's hull (`shipChoices`); repairs are not seen.

**Gallery**: particles are the sum of what happened before the frame, so scenes with content replay a short synthetic history through the same code with a seeded RNG (`web/src/gallery/vfxScenes.ts`). New scenes `explosion` (a Hauler blowing up 0.2 s ago, a burning Tinker) and `particles-stress` (an explosion every frame, 120 bolts with trails, four hulls at boost: 21 900 live particles). `gallery.mjs --vfx off` (`?vfx=off`) renders the pre-VQ5 mesh effects; the PASS line shows the live particle count. The browser smoke requires the `particles` and `dust motes` pipelines.

**Measured** (gallery, RTX 5080, Chrome 154, 1920x1080, "high", GPU timestamps): particles pass 0.13-0.19 ms at 21 800-21 900 live particles over several runs (`particles-stress`; whole frame 0.33-0.37 ms), 0.02-0.03 ms in the fight, explosion and lineup scenes (200-350 particles plus 1 500 motes), 0.00 ms in empty scenes. A smoke-only worst case (two explosions per frame, 15 000 mostly large smoke puffs) took 1.12 ms before the half-resolution split and 0.40 ms after. "medium" 0.11 ms (16 000), "low" 0.05 ms (8 200). The reference mid GPU has about 1/5 of the fill rate and bandwidth, so the stress scene is estimated at 0.8-1.0 ms (budget 1.0) and normal fights at 0.1-0.2 ms; a measurement on that class is open (as for VQ2 and VQ4). CPU: effect bookkeeping and packing run in the frame loop (a few thousand spawns per second in a busy fight).

**Deviations from the plan**:
- No compute update pass: particle motion is analytic (spawn state, drag, lifetime), which needs no per-frame GPU work and no read-back; spawns are written once.
- No per-emitter sorting: a single premultiplied blend (additive light writes alpha 0); flames are partly opaque, which keeps fireballs from summing to a flat white disc. Smoke-over-fire order follows spawn order.
- Cloak refraction shimmer is not done (it needs a copy of the scene colour; a later package with the scene copy, e.g. VQ7's shafts, can add it); a cloaked ship shows a faint shimmer of glints. Done in VQP (`renderer/cloak.ts`).
- The death explosion does not throw the VQ4 wreck parts themselves (the wreck is a replicated entity the VQ4 meshes draw); it throws hull-shard sprites and the fresh wreck smoulders.
- Explosion size per shape and visual scale, not the weapon's splash radius (the client does not know it; ls-sim is out of scope).
- "low" keeps particles (8 192 slots, 0.35 density, no motes); the pre-VQ5 mesh effects stay behind `?vfx=off` (`Renderer.create(canvas, { particles: false })`) instead of `RenderQuality.low`. The gallery shows the low preset without errors (`--quality low`).

**Shared files touched** (minimal, additive): `renderer.ts` (the `particles` field and option, depth texture `TEXTURE_BINDING` and stored, the particle encode between scene and post), `quality.ts` (`particles` per preset), `game/scene.ts` (`vfx` extra: skip bolt meshes, mesh trails, glow shells, flash spheres; fainter shield bubble), `main.ts` (create, update, reset and clear the `Vfx`; shake), `gallery/gallery.ts` (vfx flag, simulation, live count), `gallery/scenes.ts` (two scenes), `tools/browser-smoke/gallery.mjs` (`--vfx`, particle count), `tools/browser-smoke/smoke.mjs` (required pipelines), `renderer/post.test.ts` (preset test ignores the particle field), `crates/ls-wasm/src/{game.rs,mechanics.rs}` (hit cues).

**Tests**: `web/src/renderer/particles.test.ts` (packing, integer flags, the two rings, wrapping uploads, live counts, immediates cap, sprite atlas shapes and mips, WGSL entry points, presets), `web/src/game/vfx.test.ts` (shape table, scene without mesh effects, muzzle once per volley and not on the first frame, trails continuous, impact on disappearance, rocket explosion with delayed smoke and shake, range limits, shield ripple placement, death and respawn, damage smoke threshold and healing on respawn, engine mounts per hull and boost plume, cloaked ships without flames, beams, shockwaves, pickups, seeded determinism and density, effect lights), `web/src/gallery/vfxScenes.test.ts` (fight history counts, explosion scene, the stress scene keeps about 20 k alive without evicting smoke).

## 5. Notes for package authors

- The terrain vertex attribute layout (`team` u32: team, surface, material, paint colour) is documented in `contracts.md` "Chunk meshes". VQ3a and VQ3b add attributes (AO and material weights) on the client side of that contract. Update the doc in the same change.
- Keep emissive values in physical HDR units after VQ2 (for example bolt cores at 4–8, explosion flash peak 20, paint glow 0.3) and let exposure and bloom do the work. Do not clamp to 1 in shaders any more.
- Particles and lights are cosmetic and may use `Math.random`. Never feed anything back into the sim or prediction.
- Each package keeps the old path behind `RenderQuality.low` until the gallery shows parity, so a GPU without `timestamp-query` or with low limits still runs.
